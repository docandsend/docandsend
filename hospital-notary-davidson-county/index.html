The Hospital Notarization Process

How bedside notarization works.

1
Contact & Coordinate
Call or text to schedule

Provide hospital name, patient location (room number if available), and type of document. I coordinate timing with you and hospital staff to minimize disruption to patient care.

2
Hospital Visit
Professional & respectful arrival

I arrive at the scheduled time, check in with nursing staff, and follow hospital visitor protocols. I work around patient care schedules, medication times, and visiting hours.

3
Bedside Notarization
Quick, professional, compassionate

At bedside, I verify identity, explain the document (if needed), witness signatures, and complete notarization. I ensure patient understanding and willingness to sign. Entire process typically takes 10-15 minutes.

Your privacy and confidentiality are protected.

As a notary with extensive healthcare administration background, I am fully trained in HIPAA (Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act) regulations and understand patient privacy requirements.

What This Means for You:
  • I do not disclose patient information to unauthorized individuals
  • I coordinate only with authorized family members and hospital staff
  • All documents and conversations remain confidential
  • I follow hospital protocols for patient identification and authorization
  • Medical information discussed during notarization is kept strictly private

My healthcare background ensures that your family's privacy and patient confidentiality are respected at every step of the process.

When to Call a Hospital Notary

Common situations requiring bedside notarization.

  • Unexpected hospitalization requiring immediate POA or healthcare directive execution
  • Pre-surgical procedures where advance directives or consent forms need notarization
  • Long-term care planning during extended hospital stays
  • End-of-life care decisions requiring living wills or DNR documentation
  • Family coordination when multiple family members need to execute documents together
  • Discharge planning requiring financial or legal document signatures
  • Transfer to rehabilitation or hospice care with legal document requirements
  • Estate planning discussions initiated during hospitalization
